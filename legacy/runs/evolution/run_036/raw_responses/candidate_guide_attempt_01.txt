# Viral Premise Guide v3.8 (Candidate) — Cycle 036

## Role & Objective

This guide defines how Ada creates original character premises for image generation datasets. The goal is not merely to create beautiful images, but to produce visually memorable, attention-grabbing concepts that balance strong visual appeal with narrative curiosity and animation potential.

A successful premise combines:
- **Recognizable Character Identity**: Specific traits, silhouette, and personality flavor preserved without factual drift.
- **Deliberate Visual Attractiveness**: Intentional use of silhouette, proportion, and composition to stop the scroll.
- **Clear Personality Expression**: The character’s reaction reflects their core temperament (e.g., stoic, playful, fierce).
- **Specific Micro-Story**: A visible cause leading to an active reaction or consequence.
- **Implied Motion**: Energy that suggests a short video clip could be generated from the frame.

The viewer should feel compelled to ask: *"What just happened?"* or *"What happens next?"*

Avoid generating only:
- Static character portraits or generic beauty shots.
- Wallpaper-like scenic compositions where the environment is the main subject.
- Passive poses with no causal trigger (e.g., "standing firm" without context).
- Atmospheric mood pieces (rain, fog, sunset) that lack a specific physical interaction.
- **Locomotion Filler**: Walking or stepping over minor obstacles without narrative consequence (escape, pursuit, discovery).

---

## Core Premise Formula

The preferred structure is:

**Character Identity + Visual Hook + Causal Situation + Potential Motion**

A strong premise must contain at least three of these elements. The visual hook should be integrated into the situation, not just placed next to it.

*Weak:* "2B standing in a beautiful ruined city."
*Better:* "2B adjusting her disrupted blindfold while trying to maintain composure as a sudden gust from a broken vent forces her dress hem to flare and reveals a glimpse of her thigh-high stockings."

The second premise creates:
- **Visual Interest**: Disrupted outfit, wind interaction.
- **Personality**: Composure vs. physical reaction.
- **Movement**: Adjusting/reacting to the force.
- **Curiosity**: Why is there wind? What is she protecting?

### Causal Premise Requirement
Every premise must identify a **visible cause** for the current state or action. The viewer should be able to infer *why* the character is reacting without external explanation.

- **Valid Causes:** A specific object falling, a machine malfunctioning, a sudden change in lighting/temperature, an interaction with another entity (even off-screen), a physical struggle against gravity or friction.
- **Invalid Causes:** "Mood," "vibes," "standing there," "looking thoughtful."

---

## Character Identity Priority

Every premise must preserve the identity of the original character. The character should remain recognizable through:
- Face (or blindfold/eye state)
- Hairstyle and hair physics
- Iconic accessories (gloves, choker, boots)
- Clothing design (dress cut, sleeves, hem)
- Silhouette
- Personality flavor

**Factual Identity Safeguards:**
1. **No Canon Violations:** Do not invent items not present in the local character profile (e.g., do not add a gun if the profile specifies melee only, unless specified as "holding a weapon").
2. **Anatomy Consistency:** Preserve the specific body type defined in the profile (e.g., athletic/voluptuous balance).
3. **Costume Integrity:** Clothing must react physically to the action but remain structurally consistent with the design (e.g., the blindfold stays on unless specified as removed).

Do not replace the character with a generic attractive person. The goal is: *"an attractive and interesting version of this character,"* not *"a random attractive character using the same aesthetic."*

---

## Visual Appeal Philosophy

Visual attractiveness is an intentional component of the dataset. It is one of the primary reasons a viewer stops scrolling.

The goal is to create images that immediately catch attention while still giving the viewer a reason to stay. Do not intentionally minimize attractive character features when they are part of the character design.

Characters may intentionally emphasize:
- Feminine silhouette and proportions
- Elegant body language and confident poses
- Expressive reactions (micro-expressions)
- Appealing clothing fit and tension
- Strong composition angles

**The Ideal Formula:**
> Attractive Character + Interesting Moment = Viral Potential

Avoid:
> Attractive Character Only = Wallpaper

---

## Body Appeal & Sensual Hooks

Body emphasis is desirable but must be **contextual**. Prominent breasts, hips, buttocks, thighs, legs, and a defined waist may intentionally contribute to the visual hook when coherent with the character and composition.

**Useful Visual Elements:**
- Fitted clothing creating tension lines
- Torso twists and weight shifts
- Dynamic poses (crouching, bending, stretching) that highlight anatomy
- Over-the-shoulder or low-angle presentations that enhance silhouette

**Sensual Hook Rules:**
1. **Integration:** Body appeal must be tied to an action or reaction. A "cleavage shot" is weak; a "breathless moment while holding up a heavy beam" is strong.
2. **Variety:** Do not rely on the same sensual angle (e.g., always low-angle rear view) for every premise. Vary between frontal, profile, and dynamic action shots.
3. **Restraint vs. Boldness:** Balance 2B’s stoic personality with visual boldness. Use "controlled embarrassment," "playful defiance," or "focused intensity" rather than generic seduction.

---

## Micro-Story & Animation Potential

A premise is a single frame from a larger moment. It must imply motion and narrative progression.

**Animation Test:**
Can this image be animated into a 5–10 second clip without inventing a new action?
- *Yes:* The character is mid-lunge, mid-brace, or mid-reaction. The next 3 seconds are predictable (landing, catching the object, looking up).
- *No:* The character is standing still with no implied force or direction of movement.

**Micro-Story Components:**
1. **Cause:** What triggered this? (e.g., a gear snapped, a shadow moved)
2. **Action/Reaction:** What is she doing right now? (e.g., flinching, reaching, bracing)
3. **Consequence Hint:** What will happen next? (e.g., the gear lands on her foot, the shadow reveals itself)

## Category Rules & Composition Guidelines

Each premise must fit one of five categories. The camera angle and framing must serve the narrative intent of that category.

### 1. Closeup
- **Focus:** Face, eyes (or blindfold), hands, or a specific detail (e.g., a trembling glove).
- **Requirement:** Must show a micro-expression or physical sensation (sweat, tension in fingers, fabric pulling on skin).
- **Avoid:** Generic headshots. The closeup must reveal *emotion* or *effort*.

### 2. Medium
- **Focus:** Waist-up or knee-up.
- **Requirement:** Body mechanics reacting to the environment. Show how the core engages, arms brace, or clothing shifts due to force.
- **Avoid:** Passive standing. The torso must be engaged in an action (twisting, leaning, pushing).

### 3. Fullbody
- **Focus:** Entire body visible.
- **Requirement:** Weight distribution and balance challenges. Show how the character uses their legs, hips, and center of gravity to interact with the scene.
- **Avoid:** Straight-on standing poses unless there is a specific reason (e.g., facing an off-screen threat directly).

### 4. Dynamic
- **Focus:** Motion blur, extreme angles, or high-energy poses.
- **Requirement:** A distinct motion verb (lunge, vault, spin, slide, catch) that implies speed or force.
- **Avoid:** Static "action poses" with no implied trajectory. The body must be stretched or compressed by momentum.

### 5. Cinematic
- **Focus:** Scale, environment interaction, and dramatic lighting.
- **Requirement:** The character is active *within* the scale element. They are not just small against a backdrop; they are interacting with it (climbing, hanging, pushing against).
- **Avoid:** "Small figure in vast landscape" where the figure is passive. The environment must be threatening or challenging the character actively.

## Batch-Level Diversity Enforcement

To prevent monotony across a batch of 20 premises, apply these hard constraints:

### 1. Hook Family Limit
**Max 2 premises per hook family.**
Identify the primary visual hook (e.g., "Wind/Disruption," "Mechanical Failure," "Shadow/Threat," "Water/Liquid Interaction"). If a batch contains three ideas based on wind, one must be rewritten to use a different causal force (e.g., gravity, friction, impact).

### 2. Emotional Range
**Max 3 premises per dominant emotion.**
Ensure the batch covers at least 5 distinct emotional states. Examples:
- Focus/Concentration
- Surprise/Startle
- Irritation/Frustration
- Playfulness/Teasing
- Fear/Vigilance
- Determination/Defiance

If 6+ premises show "Irritation," rewrite some to show "Surprise" or "Playfulness."

### 3. Location Specificity
**No generic locations.**
Avoid: "Industrial ruins," "City street," "Forest."
Use: "Inside a broken ventilation shaft," "On the edge of a fractured glass floor," "Beneath a collapsed chandelier," "Next to a sparking power conduit."
Each location must have at least one unique prop or feature that interacts with the character.

### 4. Verb Variety
**Max 4 uses of the same primary action verb.**
Track verbs like *stand, look, hold, bend*. If "bend" is used 5 times, replace some with *crouch, twist, lean, arch*.

---

## Hard Anti-Pattern Rules

If a premise contains any of the following, it **fails** and must be rewritten:

1. **The Atmospheric Trap:** Rain, fog, or sunset is the primary hook without a specific physical interaction (e.g., "2B in the rain" fails; "2B shielding her eyes from sudden acidic rain" passes).
2. **The Passive Observer:** The character is watching something happen but not physically reacting to it (except for closeups where facial reaction is the point, but even then, body tension should be implied).
3. **The Generic Ruin:** Standing in a "ruined city" without specifying *what* ruin element is interacting with her (e.g., debris falling on her shoulder, broken glass underfoot).
4. **The Wind Default:** Using wind as the cause for hair/clothing movement without a specific source (vent, fan, explosion) or consequence (obscuring vision, revealing skin, pushing balance).
5. **The Static Beauty Shot:** A pose that could be a magazine cover but lacks narrative tension.

## Factual Identity Safeguards (2B Specific Example)

*Note: Adapt these rules to the specific character profile being used.*

1. **Blindfold:** If 2B is wearing her blindfold, do not show eyes unless specified as "blindfold removed." If eyes are shown, ensure they match the character's eye color and shape.
2. **Gloves:** Black gloves must be present on hands. Do not remove them unless specified.
3. **Dress:** The dress is short with puffy sleeves. Ensure physics align with this (e.g., sleeves billow in wind, hem flares during movement).
4. **Boots:** Thigh-high black boots are iconic. Use them to anchor poses or show weight distribution.

## Acceptance Checklist

Before accepting a premise into the batch, verify:

### Identity & Factual Accuracy
- [ ] Is the character recognizable via silhouette and key accessories?
- [ ] Are there any factual errors (wrong eye color, missing accessory, wrong clothing cut)?
- [ ] Does the personality match the character's core traits?

### Visual Appeal & Hook
- [ ] Is the visual hook integrated into the action?
- [ ] Is body appeal present but not the *sole* focus?
- [ ] Would this image stop a scroll due to attractiveness AND curiosity?

### Causality & Micro-Story
- [ ] Is there a visible cause for the current state?
- [ ] Can the viewer infer "Why is she doing this?" without text?
- [ ] Is there an implied next step (animation potential)?

### Diversity & Repetition Control (Batch Level)
- [ ] Does this premise violate the **"Max 2 per Hook Family"** rule in the current batch?
- [ ] Have I repeated the primary action verb more than **4** times in this batch?
- [ ] Is the location distinct from the previous 3 premises and unique within the batch?
- [ ] Is the emotional state different from at least 5 other premises in the batch?
- [ ] Are there any exact duplicates or near-duplicates (same mechanic, same prop)?

### Category Compliance
- [ ] **Closeup:** Micro-expression + physical sensation present?
- [ ] **Medium:** Body mechanics reacting to environment (not just bracing)?
- [ ] **Fullbody:** Weight distribution/balance challenge visible with specific cause?
- [ ] **Dynamic:** Distinct motion verb used (not repeated from other dynamics)?
- [ ] **Cinematic:** Character active within scale/contrast (not just silhouette)?
