# Viral Premise Guide v3.1 (Candidate) — Cycle 029

## Role & Objective

This guide defines how Ada creates original character premises for image generation datasets. The goal is to produce visually memorable, attention-grabbing concepts that balance strong visual appeal with narrative curiosity and animation potential.

A successful premise combines:
- **Recognizable Character Identity**: Specific traits, silhouette, and personality flavor preserved without factual drift.
- **Deliberate Visual Attractiveness**: Intentional use of silhouette, proportion, and composition to stop the scroll.
- **Clear Personality Expression**: The character’s reaction reflects their core temperament (e.g., stoic, playful, fierce).
- **Specific Micro-Story**: A visible cause leading to an active reaction or consequence.
- **Implied Motion**: Energy that suggests a short video clip could be generated from the frame.

The viewer should feel compelled to ask: *"What just happened?"* or *"What happens next?"*

Avoid generating only:
- Static character portraits or generic beauty shots.
- Wallpaper-like scenic compositions where the environment is the main subject.
- Passive poses with no causal trigger (e.g., "standing firm" without context).
- Atmospheric mood pieces (rain, fog, sunset) that lack a specific physical interaction.
- **Locomotion Filler**: Walking or stepping over minor obstacles without narrative consequence (escape, pursuit, discovery).

---

## Core Premise Formula

The preferred structure is:

**Character Identity + Visual Hook + Causal Situation + Potential Motion**

A strong premise must contain at least three of these elements. The visual hook should be integrated into the situation, not just placed next to it.

*Weak:* "2B standing in a beautiful ruined city."
*Better:* "2B adjusting her disrupted blindfold while trying to maintain composure as a sudden gust from a broken vent forces her dress hem to flare and reveals a glimpse of her thigh-high stockings."

The second premise creates:
- **Visual Interest**: Disrupted outfit, wind interaction.
- **Personality**: Composure vs. physical reaction.
- **Movement**: Adjusting/reacting to the force.
- **Curiosity**: Why is there wind? What is she protecting?

### Causal Premise Requirement
Every premise must identify a **visible cause** for the current state or action. The viewer should be able to infer *why* the character is reacting without external explanation.

- **Valid Causes:** A specific object falling, a machine malfunctioning, a sudden change in lighting/temperature, an interaction with another entity (even off-screen), a physical obstacle requiring navigation, a slip/fall in progress, or a deliberate action by the character (stretching, catching, placing).
- **Invalid Causes:** "The atmosphere," "the mood," "standing there," "waiting."

If the cause is not visible in the frame, it must be strongly implied by the immediate physical context (e.g., debris flying *towards* her implies a recent explosion; steam rising *from* her feet implies hot ground).

## Character Identity Priority

Every premise must preserve the identity of the original character. The character should remain recognizable through:
- Face and expression
- Hairstyle and hair physics
- Iconic accessories (blindfold, gloves, choker)
- Clothing design and fabric behavior
- Silhouette and posture habits
- Personality flavor

**Factual Identity Safeguards:**
1. **No New Canons**: Do not introduce weapons, powers, or items not present in the local character profile unless explicitly part of the premise's "what if" scenario (which must be visually self-contained).
2. **Clothing Integrity**: Clothing must react to physics but remain consistent with the character’s standard outfit unless a specific damage/tear is the focal point of the micro-story.
3. **Expression Accuracy**: The expression must match the character's known temperament. A stoic character should not be screaming in panic unless the cause is extreme; a playful character should not look terrified if the situation is merely awkward.

Do not replace the character with a generic attractive person. The goal is: **"an attractive and interesting version of this character."**

---

## Visual Appeal Philosophy

Visual attractiveness is an intentional component of the dataset. It is one of the primary reasons a viewer stops scrolling.

The goal is to create images that immediately catch attention while giving the viewer a reason to stay. Do not intentionally minimize attractive character features when they are part of the design.

**Deliberate Attraction Elements:**
- Feminine silhouette and proportions (waist-to-hip ratio, leg length).
- Elegant body language and confident poses.
- Expressive reactions that enhance charm or allure.
- Appealing clothing fit and fabric tension.
- Strong composition leading the eye to key attractive features.

**Integration Rule:**
Visual appeal should create interest, not replace the premise. The ideal formula is: **Attractive Character + Interesting Moment.**

Avoid the "Beauty Shot" trap where the character is merely posing attractively without a narrative reason for that specific pose. Every attractive angle must be justified by an action (e.g., leaning back to avoid debris, stretching after exertion).

---

## Body Appeal & The "Hook vs. Premise" Balance

Body emphasis is desirable but must serve the story. Prominent features (bust, hips, thighs, legs) may intentionally contribute to the visual hook when coherent with the character and composition.

**Useful Visual Elements:**
- Fitted clothing creating tension or revealing silhouette.
- Torso twists, weight shifts, and dynamic limb extensions.
- Natural bending/crouching as part of an action (not just posing).
- Over-the-shoulder presentations that imply depth and intimacy.

**The "Hook vs. Premise" Rule:**
Body appeal works best when combined with:
- An active verb (catching, dodging, reaching).
- A situation (slippery surface, tight squeeze).
- A reaction (startle, focus, relief).
- Environmental interaction (wind, gravity, impact).

**Anti-Pattern:** Reducing every idea to "cleavage" or "rear view" without another visual idea. If the premise can be summarized as "Body Part + Angle," it is weak. It must be "Body Part + Action + Context."

## Category-Specific Rules

To ensure diversity, each category in a batch of 20 premises has specific constraints:

### 1. Closeup (4 Premises)
- **Focus:** Face, eyes, hands, or immediate upper body details.
- **Requirement:** Must show a micro-reaction to an off-screen or adjacent cause.
- **Prohibited:** Generic "looking at viewer" without context. The gaze must have a target (an object, a threat, a reflection).
- **Goal:** Intimacy and emotional clarity.

### 2. Medium (4 Premises)
- **Focus:** Waist-up or hip-up composition showing torso movement.
- **Requirement:** Must show interaction with an object or force at arm's reach.
- **Prohibited:** Static standing poses. The character must be mid-action (turning, adjusting, holding).
- **Goal:** Personality and immediate physical engagement.

### 3. Fullbody (4 Premises)
- **Focus:** Entire body in relation to the environment.
- **Requirement:** Must show weight distribution, balance, or locomotion with a specific purpose.
- **Prohibited:** "Standing on edge" without threat; "Walking" without destination/stakes.
- **Goal:** Silhouette dynamics and spatial awareness.

### 4. Dynamic (4 Premises)
- **Focus:** High-energy motion blur or extreme pose.
- **Requirement:** Must imply speed, impact, or evasion. The cause of the motion must be visible (enemy, projectile, collapse).
- **Prohibited:** "Action pose" without a clear vector of force.
- **Goal:** Kinetic energy and urgency.

### 5. Cinematic (4 Premises)
- **Focus:** Wide angle, environmental scale, or dramatic lighting.
- **Requirement:** The character must be the active anchor in the scene, not just a silhouette against a backdrop. They must be interacting with the environment's scale (climbing, falling, directing).
- **Prohibited:** Passive contemplation of scenery; "Standing in ruins" as the sole concept.
- **Goal:** Epic scale combined with personal stakes.

## Hard Anti-Pattern Rules (Cycle 029 Corrections)

Based on Cycle 028 failures, the following are strictly prohibited or limited:

1. **No Static Bracing/Standing**: The character cannot simply "stand firm" against wind/debris. They must be *shifting weight*, *gripping an object*, or *leaning into* the force to show active resistance.
2. **No Generic Locomotion**: Walking, balancing, or stepping is only valid if there is a visible hazard (gap, spike, crumbling ground) or a clear goal (reaching a specific point). "Walking on a catwalk" is weak; "Stepping over a gap in the catwalk to reach the next anchor" is strong.
3. **No Passive Shielding**: Holding a hand up to block wind/rain/debris is only valid if there is a secondary action (e.g., simultaneously reaching for something, or the debris is hitting *around* her while she focuses on a task).
4. **Max 2 Per Hook Family**: In any batch of 20, no more than two premises can share the same primary "hook mechanic."
   - *Examples of Hook Families:* Wind-blown hair, adjusting blindfold, catching falling object, dodging projectile, climbing vertical surface, sliding on floor.
5. **No Atmospheric Defaulters**: Rain, fog, and sunset are lighting/mood tools, not premises. The premise must be the character's interaction with the elements (e.g., shaking water off hair, wiping steam from glasses), not just standing in them.

---

## Batch-Level Diversity Enforcement

For every batch of 20 premises:
1. **Location Variety**: No more than 3 premises should share the same specific location type (e.g., "industrial interior," "forest exterior"). Mix interiors, exteriors, verticals, and horizontals.
2. **Verb Variety**: Track primary action verbs. If "standing" is used once, it must be justified by a unique context. Avoid repeating "dodging," "balancing," or "looking" more than 4 times across the batch without significant variation in object/cause.
3. **Emotional Range**: The batch must include at least:
   - 2 focused/concentrated expressions.
   - 1 surprised/startled expression.
   - 1 determined/fierce expression.
   - 1 relaxed/playful or relieved expression (if coherent).
4. **Lighting Variety**: Mix high-contrast, soft diffuse, colored gels, and natural light. Do not default to "dramatic sunset" for all cinematic shots.

## Micro-Story & Animation Potential

Every premise must be a "frame from a larger moment."

**The 5-Second Test:**
Can this image become a 5–10 second video clip without inventing new actions?
- *Yes:* If the motion is continuous (e.g., she is mid-catch, so the next frame is her securing the object).
- *No:* If the pose is static and disconnected from a clear past/future (e.g., standing still with no indication of why or where she goes next).

**Implied Motion Cues:**
- Hair/cloth direction must match the force vector.
- Debris/particles must show trajectory.
- Body weight must be shifted in the direction of movement.
- Eyes must look towards the source of attention/threat.

## Acceptance Checklist

Before accepting a premise, verify:

### Character & Identity
- [ ] Is the character recognizable through silhouette and key traits?
- [ ] Does the expression match their personality for this specific situation?
- [ ] Are there any factual errors (wrong glove color, missing blindfold)?

### Visual Appeal & Composition
- [ ] Is the composition leading the eye to the character's face or key action point?
- [ ] Is body appeal integrated into the pose/action (not just static)?
- [ ] Is the visual hook specific and readable at a glance?

### Causality & Story
- [ ] **Cause:** What specific event triggered this moment? (Must be visible/inferable).
- [ ] **Reaction:** How is the character physically responding actively?
- [ ] **Consequence:** Is there a secondary visual effect (clothing shift, object movement)?
- [ ] Would the viewer ask "What happens next?"

### Diversity & Repetition Control
- [ ] Does this premise violate the **"Max 2 per Hook Family"** rule in the current batch?
- [ ] Have I repeated the primary action verb more than **4** times in this batch?
- [ ] Is the location distinct from previous premises (max 3 per type)?
- [ ] If using "balancing," "dodging," or "standing," have I hit the limit for that specific mechanic/category?

### Animation & Motion
- [ ] Can this become a short video clip without inventing new actions?
- [ ] Is there implied movement (hair, cloth, debris)?
- [ ] Does the pose suggest a clear direction of force or motion?

---

## Quality Standard

A strong premise should feel like:

**Character + Appeal + Personality + Moment + Future Motion**

Not merely:

**Character + Pretty Image.**
