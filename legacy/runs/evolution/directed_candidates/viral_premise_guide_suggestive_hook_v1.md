# Ada Viral Premise Guide — Directed Candidate: Suggestive Visual Hook

## Purpose and priority order

Create original, non-explicit premises for characters that make the viewer pause because the character is visually compelling in a specific, suggestive situation. The frame must communicate tension, curiosity, confidence, vulnerability, or playful ambiguity without depicting sexual acts, explicit nudity, or isolated sexualized anatomy.

Apply this priority order without exception:

1. **Strong suggestive visual hook**
2. **Sensual composition, body language, and erotic tension**
3. **Character identity and personality**
4. **Micro-story and visible causality**
5. **Animation potential**

The first read of a premise must be: *"I want to see what is happening with her."* Action, danger, spectacle, and setting may support that question, but must never replace it.

## The directed premise formula

Use this structure:

**Character identity + suggestive situation + deliberate composition/body language + small visible cause + immediate consequence**

The suggestive situation is the anchor. It should work even if the environmental description is removed. The cause and consequence make it feel caught mid-moment rather than posed for the camera.

Good causes are intimate in scale and legible in-frame: a caught hem, a door closing, a dropped object, a sudden light, a loose ribbon, a spill, a cramped space, a reflected movement, an off-screen interruption implied by a visible cue, or a prop that requires a careful adjustment.

Avoid causes that automatically turn the image into an action trailer: combat, pursuit, explosions, collapsing structures, massive machinery, chasms, attacks, or generic environmental peril.

## Suggestive-hook requirements

Every premise must satisfy all of the following:

- Name one **hook source**: private/unexpected circumstance, playful tension, compositional reveal, clothing/prop interaction, confidence-versus-restraint, or viewer-curiosity.
- Describe an intentional full-frame composition: angle, distance, silhouette, negative space, foreground obstruction, reflection, or a meaningful crop. Do not crop to an isolated body part.
- Use body language that expresses a readable attitude: controlled poise, reluctant attention, teasing restraint, momentary vulnerability, private concentration, mild embarrassment, curiosity, or composed defiance.
- Make the character’s recognizable identity strengthen the hook. 
- Include one small cause and one visible consequence: fabric shifts after she catches it; a sleeve settles after she reaches; a glance follows an interruption; a prop changes her posture; a reflection changes what she notices.
- Leave a question open for the viewer. The question must concern the character’s situation, not an external disaster.

## Sensuality boundaries

Sensuality comes from posture, silhouette, wardrobe behavior, composition, contrast in personality, proximity to the environment, and the viewer’s incomplete information.

Allowed examples of direction:

- a controlled adjustment to disrupted clothing while maintaining composure;
- an unexpected private moment interrupted by a sound, shadow, or reflected movement;
- a narrow or awkward space that creates elegant, purposeful body positioning;
- a playful or guarded interaction with a curtain, jacket, chair, locker, mirror, doorway, raincoat, glove, or fallen accessory;
- confidence contrasted with a brief crack in restraint;
- a close interaction with fabric, light, water droplets, or a prop that changes the silhouette without exposing the character.

Never use explicit sexual activity, nudity, exposed genitals, fetish framing, coercion, voyeurism presented as harm, or underage-coded characters. Do not make cleavage, buttocks, thighs, or direct camera gaze the entire idea. A body detail may support the composition only when it is connected to the situation, attitude, and full character presence.

## Causality without action-trailer drift

Keep the causal rule from run_027, but reduce its scale.

- **Required:** a visible trigger, a character response, and a visible result.
- **Preferred scale:** one gesture, one object, one small change in space, or one interruption.
- **Maximum action load:** one supporting physical action per premise. It must serve the attractive hook.
- **Not allowed as primary hooks:** combat, evasive stunts, climbing, hanging, falling, explosions, industrial breakdowns, giant gears, turbines, pipes, sparks, beams, chasms, or trailer-style threat escalation.
- If a setting is industrial, it may appear in at most one premise per batch and only as a quiet, specific backdrop; machinery cannot be the source of spectacle or the main interaction.

Before accepting a premise, remove the setting and action language mentally. If the hook disappears, rewrite it around the character’s situation.

## Personality and identity

Identity is not a replacement for attraction; it is the contrast that makes the attractive moment distinctive.

- Preserve only local-profile facts. Do not invent canon details or reveal eyes through the blindfold.
- Favor controlled and composed reactions over generic flirtation.
- Show emotion through a small change: a paused hand, tightened jaw, softened posture, deliberate head turn, restrained smile, or a return to composure.
- Do not use direct camera gaze as a substitute for a situation. It is allowed at most once per batch and only when the frame also contains a visible cause and active relationship to the environment.

## Category rules

### Closeup — four premises

Frame face, shoulders, hands, or upper torso as one coherent moment. The hook is a micro-reaction or private detail, never a static beauty portrait. Use four different trigger families and four different emotional registers.

### Medium — four premises

Frame waist-up or knees-up. Give the character one purposeful interaction with clothing, a prop, furniture, a doorway, a reflective surface, or light. Do not repeat torso twist, mirror, steam, or clothing adjustment more than once across the full batch.

### Fullbody — four premises

Show the entire silhouette and a readable relationship to a compact space. Prefer entering/leaving a room, turning in a doorway, reaching for something just out of place, stepping around a spill, settling into a seat, or managing a caught garment. No balance-on-a-ledge, climbing, combat, or stunt mechanics.

### Dynamic — four premises

Use contained motion, not combat energy: a quick turn after an interruption, a dress or sleeve settling after a pivot, a short step backward from a surprise, catching a slipping accessory, closing a door, or moving through a curtain. The motion must emphasize the attractive composition, not threat.

### Cinematic — four premises

Use wide composition to make the character’s suggestive circumstance legible within a specific place: an empty corridor, dressing area, apartment threshold, rain-lit shelter, quiet rooftop garden, archive room, train compartment, or abandoned-but-intimate interior. No giant scale, industrial spectacle, vertical peril, or trailer lighting. The wide frame should heighten privacy, anticipation, or curiosity around the character.

## Batch-level diversity ledger

For 20 premises, keep a ledger before writing final text.

- Each premise receives a unique hook source, cause, location, dominant gesture, emotional register, and composition device.
- A hook family may appear only once. In particular: mirror/reflection, steam/vapor, clothing adjustment, wind, direct gaze, doorway, and seated pose each have a maximum of one per batch.
- No more than two premises may use the same wardrobe-interaction family.
- No location may repeat. Industrial setting maximum: one, and never cinematic.
- No action-trailer vocabulary: combat, enemy, attack, chase, explosion, collapse, turbine, gear, beam, chasm, or machinery failure.
- No duplicated sentence structure, cause, verb, pose, or implied question.
- At least three emotional registers must be non-intense: private concentration, playful curiosity, composed vulnerability, mild embarrassment, trust, or quiet confidence.

## Acceptance checklist

A premise is acceptable only if every answer is yes:

1. Would the attractive, suggestive character situation still be the main hook without the environment?
2. Is the character clearly recognizably herself?
3. Does body language and composition express tension without explicit content or isolated anatomy?
4. Is there one visible, small-scale cause and consequence?
5. Does motion support the hook instead of turning into combat or spectacle?
6. Is it distinct from every other premise in hook family, gesture, setting, and emotional register?
7. Would a viewer wonder what is happening next because of the character’s situation?

If any answer is no, rewrite from the suggestive situation first, then add only the minimum causal motion needed to make the moment alive.
